Product overview & system boundaries

What Axia Security does, how data flows through the platform, and who is responsible for what.

What Axia Security does

Axia Security is a data-security platform that watches the channels where your people work, such as email, chat, collaboration tools and AI coding agents. It detects when sensitive or confidential information is shared in a risky way, explains why it matters, and helps your team respond.

Axia combines your existing DLP signals with its own AI analysis. The aim is fewer false alerts and clearer, actionable cases for the people who handle them.

How data flows

  1. Ingest. Connected sources send events to Axia, using the access your administrator approved during setup. Examples: a Microsoft Purview DLP alert, an email, an AI coding agent prompt.
  2. Enrich & classify. Axia extracts the relevant content and context, for example organization relations and guidance documents you uploaded.
  3. Analyse. AI models assess whether sensitive information was exposed and how severe it is. Models are accessed through Amazon Bedrock.
  4. Case. Relevant findings become cases in the Axia web application.
  5. Respond. Your team investigates, assigns, opens tickets and takes the response actions your administrator allowed.

System boundaries

Deployment models

ModelWhere it runs
Self-hosted / dedicatedIn a dedicated AWS account (customer-owned, or Axia-managed for one customer), deployed with Terraform. See the Deployment guide.
Axia-hostedIn Axia's AWS environment, managed by Axia.

In scope of the Axia platform

  • The Axia web application and API, including sign-in, roles, cases, Intel, Act, Enforce, Connect, Users and Settings.
  • The processing services that ingest, enrich, classify and analyse events.
  • Axia's own databases, object storage and queues in the deployment's AWS account and region.

Outside the Axia platform

  • Your source systems: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, DLP tools, AI coding agents. Your identity provider (Microsoft Entra ID or Google Workspace), and your ticketing systems.
  • Users' devices and browsers.
  • For self-hosted deployments: the customer's AWS account, networking and account-level controls.
